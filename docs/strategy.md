# Automation Empire YouTube Strategy

## Channel promise
Show independent creators how to use practical AI and automation to save time, publish consistently, and build useful digital systems without pretending results are guaranteed.

## Audience
Beginner creators, solo operators, and small online businesses who want affordable, understandable AI workflows.

## Content pillars
1. **Build in public:** show the real progress, decisions, bugs and lessons while building Automation Empire.
2. **One workflow, one result:** demonstrate a single useful automation in a short video.
3. **Tool tests:** test a tool on a real task; disclose limitations, pricing and what did not work.
4. **Creator systems:** research, scripting, editing, repurposing and publishing workflows.
5. **Digital independence:** practical ways to package skills and systems into useful digital products; never promise income.

## Repeatable 27-second Short structure
- 0–2s: visual and spoken hook. Name a pain or surprising result; avoid unsupported claims.
- 2–7s: establish the creator and the problem.
- 7–18s: show one action or workflow with screen recording/B-roll.
- 18–23s: give the useful takeaway.
- 23–27s: one CTA (follow, comment a question, or watch the next build).

Target spoken copy: approximately 48–62 words at a natural energetic pace. Let the edit breathe; timing is more important than forcing every word.

## Publishing cadence
Start with 3 Shorts per week, only if the creator can sustain the recording/editing workload. Batch scripts, but record each with the creator's real voice. After 2–3 weeks, use actual retention, viewed-vs-swiped-away, comments and subscriber conversion to choose what to repeat. Do not infer performance from views alone.

## Repurposing
One approved source recording can become:
- YouTube Short (primary 9:16 edit).
- Instagram Reels / TikTok adaptations with platform-specific caption and CTA.
- A 60–90 second explainer only when extra demonstration/context is added; do not stretch a 27-second clip with filler.
- A longer tutorial outline that expands the exact workflow with steps, screen demonstration, limitations and a result check.

Keep a content ledger with topic, audience problem, source evidence, script version, recording filename, edit status, title, description, thumbnail, approvals, destination and published URL.

## Quality and integrity rules
- Use the creator's own face and real voice recording. Do not clone a voice or fabricate endorsements/testimonials.
- Use original screen recordings and licensed/owned visuals and music.
- Label demonstrations accurately. Distinguish tested results from estimates and plans.
- No guaranteed earnings, fake screenshots, fabricated metrics or misleading AI claims.
- Check spelling, captions, audio peaks, rights, links and final crop on a phone.
- Keep every item in draft until the creator approves the exact video and destination.

## Metrics to review
Track impressions, viewed-vs-swiped-away, average view duration, average percentage viewed, likes, comments, shares, subscribers gained and link clicks. Compare videos by topic and hook, not just by total views. The YouTube API currently has a quota/403 blocker, so metrics must be read from Studio manually until the integration works again.
